A Portrait of the Author

Prose built on silent, sweeping horizons.

Marjon Musta is an Albanian writer and student residing in Tirana. At fourteen, Marjon has already carved out a distinct literary approach, balancing heavy, atmospheric suspense with deeply grounded psychological portraits of character development.

Favoring natural rhythms and avoiding overly performative prose, his writing focuses on the beauty of life's "glitches"—the unpolished, authentic spaces where human agency clashes with environment. For Marjon, storytelling is a meticulous discipline, crafting characters who seek truth on their own terms.

Beyond creative writing, his hours are spent studying language mechanics, practicing the art of translation, and learning how structural clarity can shape the way we view literature as a global, shared canvas.

Intellectual Guidance

The Mentorship of Gladiola Toska

Every writer needs an anchor—someone who recognizes the raw texture of a voice before it is fully formed. For Marjon, that defining mentor is his extraordinary English teacher, Gladiola Toska.

With boundless kindness, dedication, and unwavering support, she built a sanctuary of learning where his creative talent could flourish. She challenged him to treat language with respect and precision, providing the academic foundation and confidence needed to publish his work globally.

"Her guidance taught me that a great teacher doesn't just deliver lessons; they elevate a student's inner world, letting them see the power of their own words."

Index of Shards

Chapter I: The Canopy Speaks

The trees in the valley did not sway; they waited. They were dense, dark-ribbed arches of green that seemed to swallow up the autumn wind before it could strike the cold soil. In those moments when the clouds grew heavy over Tirana's distant edge, the quietude returned, thick and unbreakable.

He kept his hands tucked deep into his pockets, listening to the small crunch of dry twigs underfoot. There was a rhythm here—a cold, mechanical heartbeat that belonged exclusively to the mountain. It was far cleaner than the performative chatter of the city streets, a simple pattern written in leaves and dark limestone.
